# Stellar Classifications

Short guide to common stellar classes.

## Spectral/luminosity combinations: normal stars

Normal stars use spectral class (`O B A F G K M`) plus luminosity class (`V`, `III`, `I`, `Ia+`, etc.). Combined, these map to familiar names such as **red dwarf**, **blue giant**, or **yellow hypergiant**.

| Combined type | Typical classification | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Blue main-sequence star | O V | Extremely hot, massive hydrogen-burning star. | Strong UV output, powerful stellar winds, short lifetime. | 10 Lacertae |
| Blue-white main-sequence star | B V | Hot hydrogen-burning star, less extreme than O-type. | Often rapid rotator; some show Balmer emission as Be stars. | Spica B |
| White main-sequence star | A V | Bright hydrogen-burning star with strong hydrogen lines. | Many show metal-line or magnetic chemical peculiarities. | Sirius A |
| Yellow-white main-sequence star | F V | Moderately hot hydrogen-burning star. | Thin surface convection zone; common planet-host class. | Procyon A |
| Yellow main-sequence star | G V | Sun-like hydrogen-burning star. | Magnetic cycles, starspots, chromospheric activity. | Sun |
| Orange main-sequence star | K V | Cool, long-lived hydrogen-burning star. | Long stable lifetimes; frequent exoplanet targets. | Epsilon Eridani |
| Red dwarf | M V | Cool, faint, very long-lived hydrogen-burning star. | Frequent flares, strong magnetic activity, fully convective at low mass. | Proxima Centauri |
| Blue giant | O/B III | Evolved hot star above main sequence. | Strong winds, high mass loss, often variable. | Meissa |
| Red giant | K/M III | Expanded evolved star with cool surface. | Large radius, pulsation, molecular bands, mass loss. | Aldebaran |
| Blue supergiant | O/B I | Very luminous evolved massive star. | Unstable winds, strong line emission, possible LBV link. | Rigel |
| Red supergiant | K/M I | Huge cool evolved massive star. | Dusty mass loss, semi-regular variability, possible supernova progenitor. | Betelgeuse |
| Blue hypergiant | O/B Ia+ | Among most luminous hot stars. | Extreme winds, heavy mass loss, spectral instability. | Zeta1 Scorpii |
| Yellow hypergiant | F/G Ia+ | Very luminous unstable evolved star. | Pulsation, eruptions, large dusty shells. | Rho Cassiopeiae |

## Wolf-Rayet stars

Wolf-Rayet stars are evolved massive stars with exposed inner layers and dense, fast winds. Spectra are dominated by broad emission lines rather than normal absorption lines.

| Type | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| WN | Nitrogen-sequence Wolf-Rayet star. | Strong helium and nitrogen emission; hydrogen may be weak or absent. | WR 134 |
| WC | Carbon-sequence Wolf-Rayet star. | Strong carbon and helium emission; advanced stripped-core stage. | WR 135 |
| WO | Oxygen-sequence Wolf-Rayet star. | Very rare, very hot, oxygen-rich surface. | WR 102 |

## L, Y, and T dwarfs

L, T, and Y dwarfs are very cool low-mass stars or brown dwarfs. Many cannot sustain stable hydrogen fusion.

| Type | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| L dwarf | Cool object between late M dwarfs and T dwarfs. | Metal hydrides, alkali lines, dusty/cloudy atmospheres. | 2MASS J07464256+2000321 |
| T dwarf | Cooler methane dwarf. | Strong methane absorption; cloud clearing common. | Gliese 229B |
| Y dwarf | Coldest brown-dwarf class. | Water, methane, ammonia features; planet-like temperatures. | WISE 0855−0714 |

## Carbon stars

Carbon stars have more carbon than oxygen in their atmospheres, allowing carbon-bearing molecules to dominate their spectra.

| Type | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-N carbon star | Cool luminous asymptotic giant branch carbon star. | Strong C2 and CN bands; often red and dusty. | Y Canum Venaticorum(La Superba) |
| C-R carbon star | Warmer classical carbon giant. | Strong carbon bands but less dusty than C-N stars. | R Leporis |
| CH star | Metal-poor carbon-enhanced giant. | Often binary; carbon likely transferred from former AGB companion. | HD 26 |
| R Coronae Borealis star | Hydrogen-poor carbon-rich supergiant. | Sudden deep fading events from carbon dust formation. | R Coronae Borealis |

## White dwarfs and extended classes

White dwarfs are compact stellar remnants supported by electron degeneracy pressure. Spectral type depends mostly on surface composition and temperature.

| Type | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| DA | Hydrogen-atmosphere white dwarf. | Strong hydrogen Balmer lines; most common white dwarf type. | Sirius B |
| DB | Helium-atmosphere white dwarf. | Neutral helium lines; little or no hydrogen visible. | GD 358 |
| DO | Very hot helium-rich white dwarf. | Ionized helium lines; early cooling stage. | KPD 0005+5106 |
| DQ | Carbon-feature white dwarf. | Atomic or molecular carbon bands from dredge-up. | GJ 1117 |
| DZ | Metal-line white dwarf. | Polluted by rocky debris or accretion from surroundings. | Van Maanen 2 |
| DC | Featureless-spectrum white dwarf. | Lines too weak or absent, usually cooler objects. | WD 0343+247 |
| DAZ/DBZ | Hydrogen- or helium-atmosphere white dwarf with metal pollution. | Evidence for accreted planetary material. | G29-38 |
| Magnetic white dwarf | White dwarf with strong magnetic field. | Zeeman-split lines, polarized light, altered accretion behavior. | Grw +70 8247 |
| Pulsating white dwarf | Variable white dwarf with non-radial pulsations. | Useful for asteroseismology. | ZZ Ceti |

## Luminous blue variables

Luminous blue variables are very massive, unstable evolved stars near the Eddington limit. They vary in brightness, temperature, wind strength, and mass-loss rate.

| Type | Short explanation | Peculiarities | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical LBV | Very luminous unstable blue supergiant or hypergiant. | S Doradus cycles, dense winds, possible giant eruptions. | S Doradus |
| Giant-eruption LBV | LBV known for extreme historical outburst. | Ejects solar masses of material; may form nebulae. | Eta Carinae |
| Candidate LBV | Object with LBV-like luminosity and spectrum but incomplete eruption record. | Often shows strong emission lines and circumstellar dust. | P Cygni |
